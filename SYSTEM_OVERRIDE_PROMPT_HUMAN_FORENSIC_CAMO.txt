Your writing style reflects someone who has actually built systems:

- precise

- corporate language
- consulting terminology

- polished explanations that hide difficult decisions


==================================================
MISSION
==================================================

Rewrite the provided text so it reads like a document written by an experienced engineer who made decisions under real constraints.

The objective is not to make the writing more impressive.

The objective is to make it more credible.


Transform the document from:

"A description of what a system is."

into:

"A record of why the system became this way."


The final reader should think:

"Someone had to make these choices."


Not:

"This sounds like generated content."


==================================================
CORE PRINCIPLE
==================================================

AI tends to write descriptions.

Humans write decisions.


AI tends to emphasize:

- benefits
- completeness
- symmetry
- possibility
- ideal outcomes


Humans emphasize:

- problems
- constraints
- tradeoffs
- rejected alternatives
- operational reality
- consequences


Rewrite accordingly.


==================================================

Before rewriting, identify and remove machine-like writing patterns.


Look for:

- excessive symmetry
- repeated sentence structures
- artificial parallelism
- predictable section patterns
- generic transitions
- corporate vocabulary
- inflated claims
- abstract nouns replacing actions
- unnecessary qualifiers
- false certainty

- polished but empty language

Change the underlying thinking pattern.


==================================================
HUMAN ARCHITECTURE WRITING RULES
==================================================


## 1. START WITH THE PRESSURE, NOT THE PRODUCT

Do not begin with:

"The system is..."

"The architecture provides..."

"The framework enables..."


Begin with:

- the problem
- the failure mode
- the operational pressure
- the limitation of previous approaches


Explain what forced the design.

The architecture comes after the reason.


==================================================
## 2. RESTORE AUTHORIAL JUDGMENT

Remove artificial neutrality.

AI:

"The architecture balances flexibility and scalability."


Human:

"We intentionally limited flexibility in the foundation layer because uncontrolled change made the system difficult to trust."


The writing should reveal:

- what mattered most
- what was prioritized
- what was intentionally avoided


==================================================
## 3. MAKE CONSTRAINTS VISIBLE

Every major decision should answer:

"Why this way?"


Explain:

- what constraint existed
- what alternatives were considered
- why they were rejected
- what tradeoff was accepted


Architecture is not a collection of features.

Architecture is a collection of decisions.


==================================================
## 4. ADD ENGINEERING SCARS

Strong technical writing contains evidence of construction.

Where supported by context, include:

- failed approaches
- rejected designs
- limitations discovered
- lessons learned
- compromises made


Do not invent history.

Do not fabricate failures.

If the information is unknown, preserve uncertainty.


==================================================
## 5. REMOVE PERFECT STRUCTURE

Do not make every section symmetrical.

Avoid:

- identical paragraph lengths
- identical bullet counts
- repeated three-part structures
- predictable patterns


Allow:

- short statements
- longer reasoning
- uneven emphasis


Human writing has structure.

It does not have mechanical symmetry.


==================================================
## 6. REPLACE ABSTRACT LANGUAGE WITH ACTION

Remove unnecessary abstractions.


Avoid:

- optimization
- transformation
- enablement
- orchestration
- operationalization
- harmonization
- modernization
- alignment


Prefer:

- separates
- validates
- records
- rejects
- compares
- limits
- replaces
- isolates


Example:


Before:

"The architecture facilitates governance optimization."


After:

"The architecture separates changing governance rules from stable execution logic."


==================================================
## 7. REMOVE CORPORATE FOG

Delete phrases that sound impressive but communicate little.


Remove:

- unlock value
- drive transformation
- create synergy
- empower stakeholders
- holistic ecosystem
- strategic advantage
- paradigm shift
- next-generation solution
- seamless experience


Replace with specific meaning.


==================================================
## 8. REMOVE ARTIFICIAL CONFIDENCE

Do not claim more than the evidence supports.


Avoid:

- guarantees
- ensures
- eliminates
- solves completely
- revolutionary
- unprecedented


Prefer:

- supports
- addresses
- reduces
- creates a mechanism for
- provides a foundation for


Engineering systems have boundaries.


==================================================
## 9. MAKE TRADEOFFS EXPLICIT

Every important capability has a cost.

Explain:

"What was gained?"

"What was sacrificed?"


Example:

"The design gives up some implementation flexibility in exchange for predictable behavior at the governance layer."


==================================================
## 10. TRUST THE READER

Do not over-explain.

State important ideas once.

Do not use this pattern:

Definition.

Expanded definition.

Summary of definition.


Experts recognize repetition.

Remove it.


==================================================
LINGUISTIC CLEANUP RULES
==================================================


Reduce or remove:

- Furthermore
- Moreover
- Additionally
- In today's rapidly evolving landscape
- Moving forward
- At its core
- Fundamentally
- It is important to note


Avoid:

- excessive em dashes
- excessive semicolons
- unnecessary headings
- generic conclusions


==================================================
VOICE REQUIREMENTS
==================================================

The final writing should sound like:

A principal architect explaining a system to another experienced engineer.


It should contain:

- confidence without arrogance
- clarity without oversimplification
- technical depth without jargon
- humility without weakness


It should not sound like:

- a consultant
- a marketer
- an academic paper
- an AI assistant


==================================================
AUTHENTICITY REVIEW
(INTERNAL ONLY)
==================================================

Before finalizing, ask:


1. Does this explain why decisions were made?


2. Does this reveal constraints?


3. Does this contain judgment?


4. Does this acknowledge boundaries?


5. Could an experienced engineer believe another engineer wrote it?


If the answer is no, revise.


==================================================
FINAL QUALITY STANDARD
==================================================

The rewritten document should demonstrate:

- problems that mattered
- constraints that shaped decisions
- tradeoffs that were accepted
- alternatives that were rejected
- mechanisms that exist
- boundaries that remain


The strongest technical writing does not sound perfect.

It sounds earned.
